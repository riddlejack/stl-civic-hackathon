## 4. What the kickoff established

### Purpose, scope, and freedom to choose

**T1:** Ben said the challenges concern real problems the city is trying to solve, welcomed use of public data, and said compelling submissions would be taken seriously. Mike emphasized building something the city could adopt, or at least placing concrete resident proposals before the mayor. He encouraged thinking through implementation, relevant departments, and community involvement instead of stopping at an app concept.

Mike expressly allowed participants to propose their own neighborhood/community problems, build alone or with a team, and work on more than one project. The city’s initial suggestions are therefore starting points, not an exclusive list. He welcomed large ambitions that could later be narrowed; a team did not need to perform every part of an eventual real-world program itself. Forestry coordination, planting support, and volunteer activation were examples, not promised resources.

**Published eligibility:** UMSL explicitly describes virtual participation from anywhere in the United States and permits participants to identify their own community problems. Chicago residence does not conflict with that published eligibility. The official page advertises K–12, University, and Community divisions. [UMSL announcement](https://blogs.umsl.edu/news/2026/09/28/missouri-innovation-week/), [hackathon page](https://innovateusa.org/hackathon.html)

**Open distinction:** nationwide participant eligibility is established; the exact treatment of a project serving a location outside St. Louis/Missouri for city review, recognition, or adoption is not specified in a formal rulebook. The kickoff’s stakeholder discussion centered on St. Louis.

### What constitutes a useful result

**T1:** Mike repeatedly emphasized why a problem matters, the personal/community story behind it, and a route toward actual use. He contrasted the event’s civic purpose with selecting only the most technically skilled group. Daniel’s team similarly defined success as a city, county, or regional champion implementing something that improves residents’ lives. Daniel’s statement is his team’s criterion; it is not a published scoring rubric.

Open-source, already-built solutions that the city could deploy were described favorably. This is an adoption preference and organizer intent, not evidence of an established deployment agreement, maintenance budget, procurement path, or mandatory software license.

### AI tools and collaboration

**T1:** Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity Computer were discussed positively. Mike described using different systems for different tasks, including image generation, and explicitly discussed agents building while people do other work. No exclusive vendor or required development platform was named in the excerpt. Formal rules concerning existing code, external assistance, proprietary services, or code ownership remain open.

Several people raised hands seeking teammates. Mike asked participants to share skills and promised follow-up connections. Daniel said his team was open to collaborators. An attendee offered CAD modeling/drafting assistance. Mike identified a participating UMSL geospatial colleague named Zack as a Part 107 pilot; neither the full identity nor that credential was independently verified here.

Mike asked whether anyone objected to ideas being shared with the community; no objection appears in the excerpt. That exchange is not an IP assignment, an open-source license, or blanket permission to publish every future artifact.

## 7. Public city context and data found after the meeting

### CityStat — likely the resource Ben mentioned

[CityStat St. Louis](https://www.stlouis-mo.gov/citystat/) tracks municipal service performance across departments. Its categories include tree removal, vacant-lot mowing, CSB requests, potholes, refuse/recycling, and other operations. This is a strong match to Ben saying he had posted “our city stat,” but **the exact link he shared cannot be confirmed without the meeting chat**.

- [Parks/Forestry CityStat](https://www.stlouis-mo.gov/government/departments/mayor/initiatives/citystat/parks/index.cfm): hazard-tree removal, vacant-parcel maintenance, and service completion measures.
- [Street Department CityStat](https://www.stlouis-mo.gov/government/departments/mayor/initiatives/citystat/street/index.cfm): pothole/debris dashboards and related street services.

These pages can help establish existing workflows and outcomes the city measures. Dashboard publication does not establish an exportable, current, granular dataset suitable for a particular build.
