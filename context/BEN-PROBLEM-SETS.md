## The City's challenges, in the document's words (paraphrased where long)

"For those of you who are really data savvy," three geospatial/open-data
challenges:

1. **Tree canopy and green space.** "Helping us better understand our current
   tree canopy and green space distribution across the city so that we know
   where we need to invest in greening efforts." Problem Set #1 "Neighborhood
   Revitalization Through Green Infrastructure and Tree Canopy": cites the
   Urban Greening Program ($13.5 million for demolition of vacant buildings to
   remove impervious surface and reduce combined sewer overflows), the tree
   inventory, "hundreds of trees" lost in the tornado zone, "a significant
   number of vacant lots, especially (but not only) in North St. Louis", and
   Cleveland and Baltimore as examples. Ask: "identify gaps ... determine which
   corridors or neighborhoods would be the best candidates for pocket parks,
   tree planting, green infrastructure development, and other projects." Data
   listed: St. Louis GIS Tree Inventory.
2. **Unhoused population.** "Helping us understand our unhoused population so
   that we can better connect them with services ... how many unhoused
   individuals we have in the city, where they are, and how to know which
   resources they need." Problem Set #2: homelessness up 25% 2020–2025 (Peter &
   Paul); **"In 2026, 1,790 St. Louis city residents were identified as
   unhoused, with 323 lacking shelter entirely"**; vacant buildings or cars used
   as temporary housing are "difficult for the city to see, track, and reach";
   ask: where people are living or camping, "where dangerous conditions may be
   emerging", and how to target outreach. Data: Point in Time Count (Institute
   for Community Alliances).
3. **Illegal dumping.** "helping us better track illegal dumping and littering
   across the city so that we can prevent it." Problem Set #3: fines raised
   $500 → $1,000 in 2022; cameras "often lack critical information necessary to
   identify the offender"; asks for support to "better identify and track down
   perpetrators" and to "proactively identify hotspots of dumping or likely
   areas/times that offenders frequent to target preventative action". Data
   tip: the city's open 311 data, PROBLEMCODE = "Illegal dumping"; dumping can
   also be reported to police. Links: First Alert 4 (Aug 2025), Camden cameras
   and drones (Sep 2026), Oakland drones (Sep 2026).

"For those of you who are more design-oriented," two questions:

4. **Connect residents with one another.** "if an elderly woman on a block
   needs help mowing her lawn, or a neighborhood community garden needs
   volunteers on a Saturday morning, how might they connect with one another?"
   For neighbours or neighbourhood associations, "with possible connections to
   existing City resources or platforms, like our Citizens' Service Bureau."
5. **Communicating service delivery.** "how can the city better communicate
   how we're doing on delivering key city services? Take a look at our current
   CityStat webpage and let us know what resonates with you, and what doesn't.
   How might you structure or communicate this information differently?"

"On these last two questions, we're especially interested in hearing from our
young people in the room."

"Mike has more information about each of these challenges ... Regardless of
what you choose to tackle, we're just excited that you're here."
