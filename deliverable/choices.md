## Ten choices that make this trustworthy

1. **No AI in the product, on purpose.** AI helped one person audit 1.5 million requests and draft the closure classes; the product itself is plain SQL with template text. The city's AI policy (ITSA #2026-001, July 2026) bars staff from any AI tool ITSA has not approved, so this is something the city can run without that approval. _(record: `adoption-no-ai-in-product`, `presentation-say-where-ai-is`)_
2. **Graded against the city's own promise.** Every 311 request carries the date the city itself promises (3 days for a streetlight, 5 for a tow, usually 14 for a vacant lot). We grade against that, not against a target we picked. _(record: `method-promise-date`)_
3. **'Closed' read in the city's own words, in a table anyone can overrule.** We separate 'work completed' from 'referred', 'scheduled' and 'work order created' using the exact closing text, and publish every mapping so the city can disagree line by line. We show the headline two ways so it does not rest on one debatable class. _(record: `method-closure-classes`, `adoption-reviewable-classes`, `method-conservative-handoff`)_
4. **Fairness measured like for like, including against our own headlines.** Our first headline compared a quick desk referral in the southwest with field work in the north. We now compare only the same request type closed with the same words, from residents, before and after the tornado, against thresholds fixed in advance. What survives is the wait for the city's first act (a towed car is tagged in 12 days in the southwest and 37 in the north, against a promise of 5); since the May 2025 tornado, finished work mostly shows no gap; and the streetlight comparison we planned to lead with failed the test, so it was dropped. _(record: `method-like-for-like`, `correction-ward-gap-inflated`, `n3-single-light-not-robust`, `n3-decomposition`, `method-resident-channels`, `method-tornado-split`)_
5. **Blocks, not addresses; no personal data.** Every public page shows blocks, never a house or lot, by Jack's ruling. No field about a person enters the pipeline, and no request, work-order or parcel id appears in public. A private note about exposed data is being raised with the city through the hackathon organiser. _(record: `privacy-block-level`, `privacy-no-personal-fields`, `n1-20-pooled-routing-and-free-text`, `n1-19-no-ids-in-public-files`, `privacy-exposed-tables-unused`, `ethics-private-disclosure`)_
6. **We decline to watch people.** We do not try to identify dumping suspects (the city's surveillance ordinance requires aldermanic approval for that, and cameras elsewhere catch few), and requests about people rather than places (encampments, homeless calls, welfare checks, discrimination complaints) are never shown below ward level. _(record: `ethics-decline-perpetrators`, `ethics-no-encampment-points`, `n1-15-sensitive-codes`)_
7. **Every record checked against a second record.** Before calling the forestry billing ledger a cut record we checked it against CityStat's own mowing count (7,545 lots against 7,099), and because it posts in weekly batches we count weeks, not days. _(record: `method-validate-ledger`, `method-ledger-to-the-week`, `method-independent-record-and-falsify`)_
8. **Every number shows how we know it, and what failed is shown too.** Each figure carries its sources, joins and corrections behind a 'How we know' link, and the site lists what we tested that did not hold. _(record: `method-one-figure-registry`, `presentation-negative-results`)_
9. **Runs on what the city already has.** Public files the city already publishes, fetched by the weekly job only where the city's robots file allows, with free tools and no key, by staff it already employs. Under the city's IT rules that is an enhancement, not a procurement, and what we hand over is SQL and definitions, not a hosted app. _(record: `adoption-enhancement-route`, `adoption-sql-and-definitions`, `data-robots-city-site`, `adoption-no-new-staff`)_
10. **We say what it cannot do.** Data does not cut grass: crews and money are the city's binding constraints. We do not claim residents give up (we tested and found no sign of it within a year), we call repeat reports 'came back' rather than false closures, we say 'no cut billed in the ledger' rather than 'never mowed', and we say cancelled tree requests turn up in Forestry's work orders 'five times as often', not 'mostly converted'. _(record: `scope-out-of-scope-said`, `scope-calibrated-claims`, `method-refile-durable-14d`, `n5-ledger-absence-wording`, `method-tree-match-relative`)_

## Method

- The one AI-assisted step, sorting the city's closing words into 'done', 'handed off' and so on, ships as a table open to review, which a person can read and overrule line by line, so nothing in the product depends on a model. A second agent's independent rules agree on 98.4% of closures; no person has reviewed it yet. [strong; `adoption-reviewable-classes`] _(in force; open to review, not yet reviewed by a person (ten distinct texts carry about 60% of hand-off closures; about 40 type-and-text pairs for a per-type review, N8); also logged by N1, N2, N3, N4)_
- We read 'closed' through the city's own closing words and separate 'work completed' from 'referred', 'scheduled' and 'work order created', because the 311 system closes a request when a job is handed off, not when it is done. [strong; `method-closure-classes`] _(also logged by N2)_
- We test each claim against a second, independent record and run the test that could prove it wrong, and we keep the result either way. [strong; `method-independent-record-and-falsify`]
- We read the forestry ledger's dates to the week, not the day, because it posts in weekly batches (8,507 of 2026's rows are dated a Sunday). [strong; `method-ledger-to-the-week`]
- We compare wards on the same request type closed with the same words, because raw closing times mix different steps. For cave-ins the gap sits in the referral step itself (0.8 against 41.7 days), and when a referred cave-in is repaired is in no public file; across all services, type mix and closing words explain only 12% of the city's ward 1 versus ward 10 gap, so most of it is the same request, closed with the same words, later. [strong; `method-like-for-like`] _(also logged by N3)_
- We grade every request against the date the city itself promises on it (the PRJCOMPLETEDATE field: 3 days for a streetlight, 5 for a tow, usually 14 for a vacant lot), because then nobody can say we chose the bar. [strong; `method-promise-date`] _(also logged by N1, N2, N4, N5)_
- Fairness comparisons count only requests residents filed themselves, because staff-entered requests are a fifth or more of the north-side total and follow inspectors' own schedules. [strong; `method-resident-channels`]
- Before calling the billing ledger a cut record, we checked it against CityStat's own mowing count for the same weeks: 7,545 lots against 7,099. It holds at that level. [strong; `method-validate-ledger`]
- We split the ward 1 versus ward 10 gap with an exact, checkable decomposition (type mix, closing words, then speed within the same type and words), because the city's chart uses a mean, which splits with no remainder. The answer was not what we expected: mix and closing words explain about 12% of it. [strong; `n3-decomposition`] _(logged by N3)_
- The mowing claims rest on 2026, because the 2025 ledger is thin (crews went to tornado debris) and 'no cut in 2025' proves little. [moderate; `method-2026-not-2025`] _(also logged by N1, N5)_
- We link a 311 address to a parcel through MSD's parcel address file and always report how many matched (90% of vacant-lot weed requests, 93% of vacant-building ones), because the 311 file has no parcel id and the ledger has no address. [moderate; `method-address-parcel-join`] _(also logged by N1)_
- We show the hand-off share two ways (35.2%, and 25.2% if enforcement notices count as neither), so the headline does not hang on one debatable class. [moderate; `method-conservative-handoff`] _(also logged by N2, N3)_
- Only three things could rule an idea out (needing new staff, no measurable effect on residents, no real data); everything else was weighed with evidence, because Jack judged seven knock-outs too rigid. [moderate; `method-gates-vs-weights`]
- A separate agent recomputes every on-screen number from the raw files before the COO sees it. [moderate; `method-independent-check`]
- We use medians and the share done by the promised date counting open requests as not done, because averages over closed requests hide the requests that never close. [moderate; `method-medians-and-survival`] _(also logged by N1, N3)_
- We measure need by what residents did (requests filed, waits) rather than by news coverage, because attention and need point at different problems. [moderate; `method-need-is-acts`]
- Before any agent's number was passed on, the integrator recomputed it from the raw file; the one that did not match was held back. [moderate; `method-orchestrator-spot-checks`]
- We rebuilt CityStat's own figures from the public file before proposing anything new. One shared rule reproduces its published request counts within 0.2% for 8 of 9 metrics (identical in 66 of 72 metric-quarters, never off by more than 4 requests; parks 2.1%) and its percentages within 5 points in 76 of 81 quarters; where it does not match (routing, the lights clock, two Q2 pothole shares) we ask rather than accuse. [moderate; `method-reproduce-before-critique`] _(also logged by N4)_
- A ward comparison counts as robust only if it holds on two measures, with at least 100 requests a side, before and after the tornado; we set those rules before looking. [moderate; `method-robust-thresholds`] _(also logged by N3)_
- We check every ward gap before and after the May 2025 tornado, so a gap caused by recovery work is not mistaken for a standing one. [moderate; `method-tornado-split`] _(also logged by N3)_
- We lead with vacant lots, not vacant buildings, because contractors cut vacant-building lots and may not be billed into the ledger, so those figures are a lower bound. [moderate; `method-vacant-building-lower-bound`]
- The mowing look-back flags lots that went more than 90 days without a billed cut (counting to 30 September), not 42 as first specified, because the city's own cycle runs about eight weeks (median longest gap 54 days) and a 42-day rule flags 96% of lots. [moderate; `n1-22-mowing-gap-threshold`] _(logged by N1)_
- Weed requests are graded by whether the ledger shows a cut by the promised date, not by their closing words, because almost every weed closure says 'on schedule to be cut', so the words alone say nothing. [moderate; `n1-24-weeds-graded-by-ledger`] _(logged by N1)_
- Class shares are shares of closures; cancelled and open requests are reported beside them, because for some services cancellation is the main outcome (city tree requests: 8,214 of 14,007 cancelled) and folding it in would blur what 'closed' means. [moderate; `n2-closures-only-denominator`] _(logged by N2)_
- 'Converted' means only that a work order or crew ticket was created; a place in a programme queue (such as the next available planting) is a hand-off, because a work order can be joined and finished and a queue position is not published. [moderate; `n2-converted-narrow`] _(logged by N2)_
- A service counts as having a completion record only if a city file outside 311 records dated work at an address we can join; the city's pothole work-order layer does not count because it is the same requests with the same close times. [moderate; `n2-register-record-test`] _(logged by N2)_
- The cave-in referral gap (0.8 against 41.7 days) is reported as a difference in process at a line between wards 5 and 6, not as speed of repair, because referral time jumps at that line in every ward. [moderate; `n3-cavein-process-flag`] _(logged by N3)_
- We reproduced CityStat's ward chart as the mean days to close over closed requests, all channels, filed 1 Jan 2024 to 30 Jun 2026, which lands within 0.85 days per ward on average; that is the definition every comparison with the chart uses. [moderate; `n3-citystat-definition`] _(logged by N3)_

The shared rule reproduces CityStat's published counts within 0.2% for 8 of 9 metrics (identical in 66 of 72 metric-quarters, never off by more than 4 requests; parks 2.1%). [moderate; `n4-d04`] _(logged by N4)_

N1 and N3 count cancelled requests as not done, so the published measures differ: N4's scoreboard gives 57.8% closed and 19.5% done by the promised date; N1's citywide figures give 50.8% and 16.7%.
