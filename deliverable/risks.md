## The six objections

**1. "This is a redesign of our site."**
> It's one column and a weekly job behind the site you already run; the prototype has its own design and borrows CityStat's structure only for the scoreboard, so you can see where the column would go.

Backing: the site is labelled an independent prototype with no city seal or colours (`site/config.json` footer). What the city would adopt is the SQL and the definitions (`site/data/downloads/`), not our pages. Ben's written question 5 asked entrants how CityStat could communicate differently, so a page is in scope; a clone is not what we built.

**2. "Your numbers don't match ours."**
> They do where the definitions match: rebuilt from the public file, our on-time shares land within five points of CityStat's in 76 of 81 published quarters, and where they differ it's because open requests are left out and quarters are read early, which the SQL shows line by line.

Backing: {{fig:n4_repro_metrics_within5pp_C}} [8 of 9 metrics; note: 76 of 81 metric-quarters]. Early readings overstate the final share by up to {{fig:n4_drift_pothole_residential_max}} [18.5] points for residential potholes. Say the limit too: Q2 2026 residential and alley potholes still sit a few points above anything the file supports, and we do not know why (`research/chapters/N4-citystat.md`). Every one of our figures is labelled "our definition, from the public file".

**3. "This is an audit."**
> It's a measure your own team runs on your own promise dates and your own records, it shows the system working where it works, and it names no employee and grades the process, not people.

Backing: the grading uses `PRJCOMPLETEDATE`, the date the city's own system stamps on each request, not a target we chose (N4-D01). The counter-example is on the site: a land-bank lot on the 4000 block of Ashland Av, closed in minutes and cut billed four days later (`heroes.json`, lot-counterexample). No staff field reaches any output (N4-D11, N1-20). If pressed: the mayor's own transition committee recommended "audit previously closed requests to ensure action was taken". That was a recommendation to her, not her commitment, so use it only if asked.

**4. "Data doesn't cut grass."**
> Agreed, and the video says so: crews and money decide how much gets done; this shows which jobs got done, so the crews you have go where the misses are, and CityStat can make the case for more.

Backing: the binding constraints are on the city's own record (tree trimmer and mowing vacancies, unfunded mains; `research/synthesis.md` 8c). CityStat's program manager told KMOV this summer that CityStat could help make the case for more resources (reported, KMOV, 9 Jul and 14 Aug 2026; `research/mayors-office-voice.md` 5.1). The entry claims no effect on residents beyond what the records show (`deliverable/negative-results.md`: the exit and status-text tests).

**5. "We're already buying asset software."**
> Good: whatever you buy has to define done and link each request to its work order, and this is that definition, tested on your own files today, so it becomes the acceptance test for the new system.

Backing: Cityworks moves to the cloud in FY2027 (CIO at the 11 May 2026 hearing, reported); "Purchase asset management software" is in the Rams plan; EO 96 orders an asset inventory and dashboard with no date (`research/mayors-office-voice.md` 5.6). None of these defines "done" per service. If question 1 to Ben comes back "Cityworks already links completion", the answer becomes "then publish it, and here is the measure to publish".

**6. "Where is the AI?"**
> AI was the method: one person with AI agents audited 1.45 million requests, the Forestry ledger and the city's work-order samples in two days, and drafted the table that sorts closing words into "done" and "handed off"; the product runs no AI on purpose, because ITSA policy 2026-001 bars unapproved AI tools, so what ships is plain SQL and a table a person can overrule line by line.

Backing: {{fig:n1_duplicate_rows.request_ids}} [1,454,222 distinct requests, 2008 to 13 Oct 2026]. The class table is `research/chapters/N2-closure-classes.csv`, and its SQL twin, `build/N2/closure_class.sql`, agrees on all 2,992 type-and-text pairs. The pipeline calls no model (N1-17). **Open weakness:** no person has reviewed the AI-drafted class table yet (N2 admission). Ten distinct closing texts carry about 60% of hand-off closures when pooled across request types (N8: 61%); a type-by-type review means about 40 type-and-text pairs. Reviewing those before Thursday would let Jack say "reviewed" truthfully. Until then the site must not say "reviewed" (N8 finding 5).

## Weak points an informed critic could press (found tonight, updated after N8)

Ranked by how much each would hurt in front of the COO.

1. **Lot-1 drew a "cut incomplete" call on 9 September** ("Unsatisfy Cut - VLot", closed after 24 hours, "supervisor notified"). The site's receipt shows it, so storyboard A now names it in the narration rather than scrolling past it (N8 finding 1). Someone may have cut the lot without a charge reaching the ledger, so every line says "no cut billed", never "never cut". *Answer if raised:* "A cut-incomplete call is not a record of a cut. Only about three in ten such calls this year came within 30 days after a billed cut ({{fig:n5_unsat_cut_calls_2026_cut_30d_before}} [63] of {{fig:n5_unsat_cut_calls_2026}} [227]; N8's own join: 63 of 229). If a crew came, the city's own records can't show it, and that is the point." Lot-3 has the same pattern (a missed-cut call on 28 July and a cut-incomplete call on 7 August, 0 cuts billed). N5 and N8 agree within 2 on calls at lots with no cut billed all year ({{fig:n5_unsat_cut_calls_2026_no_cut_2026}} [40]; N8's join: 42). N8's earlier 61 counted lots with no cut billed before the call, not all year (N8 re-check, correction i).
2. **"The north doesn't wait longer for crews" holds since the May 2025 tornado, not before, and not for every service.** For resident requests filed since the tornado, where the words say done, potholes, refuse, damaged bins and block-wide outages show little or no gap ({{fig:n3_no_gap_done_types_after}} [6.9 vs 8.0; 1.7 vs 1.8; 7.8 vs 8.2; 0.7 vs 0.7]). Before the tornado, finished residential potholes ran 22.3 against 31.8 days and major-street potholes 15.2 against 21.4 (N8 finding 7, observed, not registered; both ratios are under the 1.5 rule). Cave-in "work completed" runs {{fig:n3_cavein_done_median_after}} [8.3 vs 23.1] days on only 35 requests in wards 1–5. The narration now says "since the tornado". *Answer if raised:* "Since the tornado, for the services most people call about, the finished-work gap is small. Before it, potholes were slower in the north too. Where we have too few cases to say, the site says so."
3. **Lot-1's promise date is 90 days; most vacant-lot requests carry 14** ({{fig:n5_weeds_lot_promise_days_2026}} [1,244 of 1,497 in 2026 carry 14 days]; N8 finding 15e). The README's list of promise dates says "14 for most vacant-lot requests". *Answer if raised:* "The city's system set 90 days on these two; we grade against whatever date it set."
4. **The towing promise share is settled: 32% against 4%** for "tagged for tow" requests, wards 1–5 against 10–14 ({{fig:n3_towing_tagged_by_promise_after}} [0.318 vs 0.037; n 1,300 / 271]; N8 conflict 1). It is the same population as the 12.0 and 36.9-day medians. No script speaks it. N3's 38% against 11% counts any closing words and is retired. If used: "tagged within the city's own five-day promise about one time in three in wards 1–5, fewer than one in twenty in wards 10–14" (the north figure rests on about 10 requests).
5. **"One in four" was the wrong number.** It mixed "added to rotation" closures in with "on schedule to be cut". For "on schedule" alone it is one in five ({{fig:n1_weeds_lot_onschedule_only_no_cut_2026}} [21.4%]; N8 finding 2). For vacant buildings the same split gives {{fig:n1_weeds_bldg_onschedule_only_no_cut_2026}} [32.2%] against the mixed 45.2%. The script and README now say one in five.
6. **Towing changed in August.** "Tagged for tow" closures fell and "vehicle towed" rose from August 2026, consistent with the close-only-when-towed change CityStat promised (inferred). The 0.9% ({{fig:n4_tow_closed_says_towed_share}} [0.9%]) covers January 2024 to June 2026. Credit the change if towing comes up; do not present the 0.9% as current practice.
7. **Q3 may be published before Thursday.** If it is, change "a quarter CityStat hasn't published" to "our weekly run had it in July".
8. **Residential pothole on-time shares have swung widely before.** CityStat's own published values were below 30% in several 2024 quarters, so Q3 2026 (24.3%) is a low after an unusually high Q2, not a first. The claim is about timing (a weekly run would have shown it in July), never "potholes collapsed" (COMMON correction 8). Source: `build/N4/citystat_comparison.csv`; those quarterly values are not registered as figures.
9. **The disclosure must not be pre-announced.** No public text may say a disclosure was made, or describe what is exposed, until Jack has sent the note (N8 finding 3). The README line now reads "[JACK: update once sent]". The site's `decisions.json` and methods page still need N6 and N9 to remove their wording.
10. **The repository cannot be published as it stands.** `build/*/private/` is not git-ignored, and the baseline commit holds request ids, parcel ids and addresses. Publish from a fresh repository with one clean commit (N8 finding 4).
11. **Two house numbers leaked through the block labeller** in the public Monday list download (two two-number labels kept a house number; fixed 01:06). N8 finding 14; the privacy scan now checks for that pattern across site/, deliverable/ and build/out/.
12. **The receipt search works only on samples.** The static site carries 200 sample receipts and the heroes, not every request. Say "every request can carry a receipt" (the database holds them all), not "type in any request".
13. **The Monday brief's own lists disagree with the rest in two places** (N8 findings 11–12). Its tree list counts open removal orders within 30 m (499) where the story uses the same address (288). Its land-bank list counts all channels (103 on 82 lots) where N5 counts resident requests (94 on 75). In the video, show only its July pothole callout, whose numbers are registered and verified.
