# N5. Hero cases for the receipt and the video

Block level throughout. Machine-readable cases: `build/N5/heroes.json`; parcel-level detail is kept out of the repository (`build/N5/private/`). Figures with derivations: `build/N5/figures.json`.

## Admission

**What I did.** I joined the city's 311 file (2025–26, one row per request id) to the Forestry billing ledger and the land-bank (LRA) inventory by parcel through MSD's parcel address file. I also matched it to the public Cityworks tree-removal sample by normalised address. From those joins I picked ten cases by stated rules: three ranked lots, a counter-example, a light pair, two tree cases and a cave-in pair. I checked each lead lot against the obvious innocent explanations: whether it is on Forestry's maintenance list, LRA offers and garden permits, cuts on the same city block, and its ledger history back to 1996. I exported aerial images of each lot's block from MSDIS. That host needs no key and has no imagery newer than 2025.

**What I did not establish.** A missing ledger charge is not proof that nobody cut the lot. The ledger is a billing record, dated in weekly batches, and lot-1 drew an "unsatisfactory cut" complaint. The 2025 ledger is thin (8,780 parcels cut, against 17,295 in 2024), so I argue from 2026 only. The aerial images are from 2022, 2024 and 2025, so they show what each block is, not the 2026 grass. The tree matches rest on a 1,500-row sample of 2,522 open removal orders and on exact address matching, so they are lower bounds. No city dataset states a licence.

**What I assumed.** Cityworks dates are UTC and 311 times are Central. Under that reading, tree-1's three timestamps line up to the minute. "Promise date" means the request's `PRJCOMPLETEDATE`, as given. For vacant-lot weeds it is 14 days on some 2026 requests and 90 on others.

**Corrections from N8 (applied 14 Oct).** Ledger histories now count distinct cut dates: the ledger repeats legacy rows, so lot-1 reads 6 and 5 cut dates in 2019 and 2020, not 12 and 10. The cut-complaint calls are spelled out in the timelines. "City block" is used where the ledger's CITYBLOCK is meant.

**Corrections I applied.** The 311 files repeat a request once per caller (16,042 ids repeated in 2025–26), so I used one row per id. That moves the earlier medians: single light out goes from 15.8 vs 42.1 days to **15.6 vs 41.0**, and cave-in "work completed" from 9.6 vs 25.1 to **8.3 vs 23.1**.

## The cases (one sentence each)

1. **The lot (lead), 1700 block of N Taylor Av, Greater Ville, Ward 12.** Three city-owned lots around N Taylor and Aldine were reported together on 2 July and again on 28 July 2026. Every request closed within minutes as "on schedule to be cut". The ledger bills cuts on the two neighbours within four weeks, but none on this lot all season, past the city's own 30 September promise date. Meanwhile 36 other parcels on its city block were billed on 15 different days. On 9 September a resident called to report the lot's cut was left incomplete ("Vacant lot cut incomplete"; closed in 24 hours, "supervisor notified"), and no cut is billed on the lot before or after. Such calls do not show a cut happened: of 227 parcel-matched ones in 2026, 63 came within 30 days after a billed cut and 40 were on lots with no cut billed all year. N8, using a wider address join, gets 229, 67 and 61. Both 2026 requests carry a 90-day promise, where 1,244 of 1,497 vacant-lot requests carry 14.
2. **The lot (second), 8100 block of Church Rd, Baden, Ward 12.** Reported four times between April and August 2026, each request closed within hours as "on schedule to be cut", and the ledger records no cut on it in 2026. It has no "cut incomplete" or "missed cut" call in 2025 or 2026, and all four requests carry the usual 14-day promise, all four passed. Its weakness is that only one other parcel on its city block was billed in 2026, so there is no "crews were next door" contrast.
3. **The lot (third), 1300 block of Arlington Av, Hamilton Heights, Ward 13.** Five weed reports in July and August 2026 each closed within a day as "on schedule". There was also a staff "missed on rotation" report (28 July) and a resident "cut incomplete" call (7 August), with no cut billed on the lot before or after. The ledger billed the lot in every year but one from 1996 to 2024 and shows nothing since. The land bank's own offers file (an offer marked accepted in January 2026) may explain why, which 311 never said.
4. **Counter-example, 4000 block of Ashland Av, Greater Ville.** Reported 11 September 2026, closed in three minutes, and the ledger bills a cut four days later: the system working. Of 331 comparable requests on land-bank lots in 2026, 38 had a cut billed within 7 days and 106 by the city's own promise date; 94 (on 75 lots) had none by 13 October.
5. **The light pair.** A single light out on the 5300 block of S Kingshighway Blvd (Ward 5), filed 29 Dec 2025 with a three-day promise, closed "work completed" in 15.5 days. The same report on the 1200 block of N Euclid Ave (Ward 10), filed 18 Dec 2025, took 40.9 days. Same words, same promise, 2.6 times the wait; both are about their ward group's median.
6. **The tree, converted (tree-1), 1200 block of Hebert St, Ward 14.** The inspection was logged done at 11:44 on 10 April 2025, and a removal work order shows a projected start at 12:26. The 311 request was cancelled at 12:27 with no words. The work order, due 14 November 2025, was still open on 13 October 2026.
7. **The tree, already queued (tree-2), 6700 block of Dale Ave, Ward 4.** A resident asked for an inspection on 12 June 2026. A priority-1 removal order had been open there for 575 days and was past its June 2025 due date. The request was cancelled two hours later with no public words, and the order was still open on 13 October.
8. **The cave-in pair.** A cave-in on the 4600 block of Dahlia Ave (Ward 2) closed in about a day as "referred to street maintenance for repair work to be scheduled". That is fast on the scoreboard, but no public file we hold says when the repair happened. One filed three days later on the 4300 block of College Ave (Ward 11) closed after 24 days as "work completed": slow on the scoreboard, but the only one of the two whose closure says the work was done.

Images: `build/N5/img/*_block_naip2024.png` (all ten heroes) are the only ones cleared for public use: NAIP 2024, USDA, public domain; legible at block scale. The MSDIS 2022, 2024 and 2025 frames of the lot blocks carry a no-redistribution clause (2022 and 2024 product metadata) or unknown terms (2025), so they are marked not cleared. All are about 175 m across, centred on the block, with no marker. The 2025 post-tornado frame of the N Taylor block shows storm debris. For the video, the lead lot works best told with its two neighbours: same reporter timing, same closing words, different ledger outcome.

## What failed or surprised me

- **"Converted to a work order" is the rare pattern.** I matched 345 blank-cancelled tree inspections to open removal orders. Only 9 were cancelled within a day of the order starting. 288 were filed while a removal order was already open, with a median age of 575 days (quartiles 260 and 1,253). The common story is "already on the backlog, nobody said so", which is why tree-2 exists.
- **"Referred" is not fast everywhere.** In wards 10–14, cave-ins closed as "referred ... to be scheduled" take a median 41.7 days (deduplicated), against 0.8 days in wards 1–5.
- **Duplicate rows matter most for cave-ins.** About 15% of their rows repeat, and the wards 10–14 "referred" median falls from 49.9 to 41.7 days once they are removed.
- **Lot candidates that looked strong had innocent explanations in the city's own files.** These were accepted LRA offers, a "fenced and paved" category, and blocks with no mowing at all. I dropped four of them.
- **The imagery is unhelpful as evidence.** MSDIS publishes years only, with no acquisition dates. It has nothing from 2026, and Sentinel-2 is too coarse for a single lot.
- **Negative results.** The trim work-order sample has no dates and dates from 2019–20, so it was not used. No 2026 aerial imagery exists on any keyless service I found. There is no public street-maintenance work-order record to close the cave-in "referred" loop.
