# R3b. What the unfinished jobs cost residents: external evidence, and cities that checked

Primary sources only, unless a row is marked **reported**. Five research agents opened the sources on 14 Oct 2026 between 12:24 and 12:51 p.m. Central. I spot-checked the figures from seven sources myself in the primary text: Moyer 2019, South 2023, USFA 2018, and the Edmonton, Dallas, Chattanooga and New Orleans audits. Their working files, with full URL logs, are in `build/R3/work/`. Figures used anywhere public are in `build/R3/figures.json`. Downloads are git-ignored under `build/R3/private/raw/`.

## Summary

**Found.**
- **Lots and buildings.** The best evidence is randomised and from one city, Philadelphia, and it covers the two jobs St. Louis 311 most often closes at a hand-off.
  - Routine mowing and trash clean-up of vacant lots cut nearby shootings by 9.2% (Moyer 2019).
  - Full remediation of abandoned houses, meaning working doors and windows, cut gun assaults by 13%, while clean-up alone did nothing (South 2023).
  - In both trials the benefit comes from the job being done and kept up. That is exactly what a closure "on schedule to be cut" or "referred to board up crew" does not record.
- **Fires.** Vacant homes burn: about 23,800 vacant residential fires a year nationally, 11,000 of them in buildings recorded as unsecured. Half consume the whole building (USFA). The federal investigation of a St. Louis firefighter's death in 2022 names a derelict, previously burned, *boarded* house as a contributing factor.
- **Dead trees.** Forest Service guidance rates them high-risk, with risk rising the longer they stand. New York's falling-limb claims rose and fell with its pruning budget.
- **Weeds and trash.** Weeds over 12 inches and bags left more than a day are CDC rat criteria, and Chicago garbage complaints track confirmed rats.
- **Streets.** Unrepaired streets and sidewalks cost New York about $100 million a year in injury claims.
- **Closing the loop.**
  - Boston residents shown a photo of finished work filed 60% more requests (Buell 2021).
  - In Kansas City, Missouri, the areas that complain least have the worst streets and the lowest satisfaction (Kontokosta & Hong 2021).
- **Other cities' auditors have measured the same pattern.**
  - New Orleans's inspector general found that 34% of closed pothole requests were closed by referral, with no tracking of the repair; only 40% were resolved.
  - Edmonton's auditor counted 39,000 tickets closed before the work was done.
  - Los Angeles closed 12,700 sidewalk requests at inspection, without repair.
  - San Diego's auditor found 19% of sampled closures inaccurate or misleading.
  - Chicago's inspector general found weed complaints closed at citation, with no field to record the outcome.
  - Dallas, Chattanooga, San Francisco and New York show the same pattern.

**Corrections to earlier notes.**
- The "−29.1% gun violence" figure is the trial's largest, compliance-adjusted, poor-neighbourhood estimate. The citywide effect of greening on gun assaults is −2.7%.
- The "NYC pruning cut emergency clean-ups 22%" figure has no primary source and is disputed. Drop it.
- The Keizer 2008 littering percentages (33% vs 69%) could be confirmed only from secondary sources.

**Could not establish.**
- Any study of a job done *late* or *not verified*, as opposed to not done at all.
- A St. Louis count of vacant-building fires, or St. Louis tree, pothole or sidewalk claims.
- Any study isolating missed refuse collection.
- NFPA's figures could be read only through Internet Archive copies, so they are labelled reported.

**How to read the transfer notes.** Every study below compares a job that was **done** with one that was **not done at all**. None measures what St. Louis's hand-off closures produce: a job done late, done less often, or not verified. So none gives a St. Louis effect size. What they bound is the stake: the size of the benefit the city's own schedule is meant to deliver, which a missed or unverified job forgoes for as long as it stays undone (inferred).

## Bounding: what these studies allow us to say about St. Louis (inferred)

- **Uncut lots.** In 2026, 156 of 729 parcel-matched lot requests closed only "on schedule to be cut" (21.4%) had no cut billed all season (N1). In the Philadelphia trial, routine mowing and clean-up was followed by about 9% fewer shootings around treated lots (95% CI 5–13%; Moyer 2019). The defensible statement:
  - each lot the city says is "on schedule" but does not cut goes without the routine upkeep that a randomised trial linked to fewer nearby shootings;
  - the forgone benefit is of that order for as long as the lot stays uncut.

  Not defensible: a count of St. Louis shootings. St. Louis lots are partly maintained (most were cut in other years), baselines differ, and no study measures a *missed* cut.
- **Open buildings.** For "referred to board up crew", a board-up was recorded within the 14-day promise for 19%, within 30 days for 36% and within 90 days for 71% (N2). In the Philadelphia trial, securing the openings carried the effect: −13% gun assaults, while clean-up alone did nothing (South 2023). The weeks between "referred" and a recorded board-up are therefore weeks without the part of the remediation that worked. Plywood is weaker than working doors and windows, so this is an upper bound on what a board-up buys.
- **Owner notices.** The doors-and-windows ordinance worked only alongside re-inspection about every 35 days, and even then the effect was small (IRR 0.96; Kondo 2015). A 311 closure at "notice to owner" with no recorded re-inspection is the weakest form of this intervention.

## The five strongest analogue figures

Ranked on three things: the design, how closely the job matches the St. Louis hand-off, and whether we read the figure in the primary source ourselves.

1. **Mowing and trash clean-up of vacant lots cut nearby shootings by 9.2%** (95% CI −13.2% to −4.8%). Philadelphia, 541 lots, cluster randomised trial (Moyer et al., *AJPH* 2019). https://doi.org/10.2105/AJPH.2018.304752
2. **Securing abandoned houses (working doors and windows) cut gun assaults by 13.1%; clean-up alone did nothing.** Philadelphia, 258 houses, cluster randomised trial (South et al., *JAMA Internal Medicine* 2023). https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2022.5460
3. **About 23,800 vacant residential building fires a year in the U.S.**, with 75 deaths, 200 injuries and $785 million in losses. 11,000 of them were in buildings recorded as vacant and unsecured, and 53% involved the entire building (U.S. Fire Administration, 2018). https://www.usfa.fema.gov/downloads/pdf/statistics/v18i9.pdf
4. **New Orleans: of closed pothole requests, 34% were closed by referral with no tracking of the repair, and only 40% were resolved** (Office of Inspector General, 2022). This is the nearest U.S. precedent to St. Louis's 35% hand-off share. https://nolaoig.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Inspection-of-NOLA-311-Pothole-Repairs.pdf
5. **Boston residents who got a photo of the finished job filed 59.8% more requests in the next 13 months.** Natural experiment, 21,786 users (Buell, Porter & Norton, *MSOM* 2021). https://doi.org/10.1287/msom.2020.0877

Runners-up:
- Edmonton's auditor found 39,000 tickets closed before the work was done (2023).
- San Diego's auditor found 19% of 224 sampled closures inaccurate or misleading (2022).
- In Kansas City, Missouri, the areas that complain least had the worst streets (2021).
- Los Angeles closed 12,700 sidewalk requests at inspection without repair (2021).

## Negative results and open items

- **No study measures a job done late, done less often, or not verified.** Every study compares done with not done, so the St. Louis transfer is an inference about a benefit forgone (see "Bounding").
- **No auditor or inspector general measured, by site visit, the share of requests closed "complete" with no work done.** San Diego's sample check is the nearest.
